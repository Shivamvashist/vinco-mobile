The plan for a winter arc app that gives you a few non-negotiables a day, proof that you are changing, and a counter that shows how much of your life the feed is eating.

Plan dated 6 October 2026

Overview

Vinco ships in two steps: the arc tracker on 2 November 2026, and the full MVP with the reel counter on 1 January 2027.

What Vinco is

An Android app for people running a 30, 60 or 90 day arc. Every day you complete four non-negotiable tasks, take a selfie, and see your progress fill in. The Vigil counts every reel and short you watch, shows it live, and steps in with a Stoic nudge at milestones.

The theme is Roman and Stoic, the look is modern, and the voice can be calm, firm or sarcastic. The only battle that matters is against yesterday's you.

Who it's for

The arc starter

18 to 25, student or first job. Has tried a winter arc before and dropped off around week two. Wants to be told what to do today.

The gym beginner

Started lifting recently and already takes mirror photos. Wants proof: the timelapse is their feature.

The scroller who knows it

Checks screen time, feels bad, changes nothing. Has tapped "ignore limit" a hundred times. The Vigil is for them.

Not for now: under-18s, people who want a full calorie tracker, and teams. Rate the app for adults.

Brand and theme

Roman in spirit, modern in use: a sleek fitness app that happens to be carved in marble, not a museum.

The name: Vinco

Latin for "I conquer," in the present tense. It is about today's battle, not a finish line. It shares a root with the tagline's "vici" and with the word invincible.

"Vincit qui patitur."

An old Latin proverb: the one who endures, conquers.

The story behind the name: Caesar conquered Gaul. Your users conquer their alarm, their phone and their excuses. Plato called conquering yourself the first and best of all victories; Seneca said ruling yourself is the greatest power.

A day in Vinco

  1. Aurora rings. It only stops when you get up and complete a mission, like photographing the sky from your balcony.
  2. First water. The alarm screen hands you straight to task one: 1 litre. One tap.
  3. Morning brief. Today's tasks, your campaign and one Stoic line, in your tone.
  4. Through the day. Tick tasks as you go. The Vigil counts reels quietly in a corner of Instagram and YouTube.
  5. Workout logged. Tap done, then speak for 15 seconds about what you trained.
  6. Selfie. Ten seconds, lined up against yesterday's photo.
  7. Evening check. One nudge, only if something is still open, plus today's reel total.
  8. Day sealed. The VINCO stamp lands, the calendar fills, the day card is ready, and your bedtime starts the sleep log.

Onboarding, under two minutes

  1. Arc length: 30, 60 or 90 days, starting today.
  2. Your orders: the four non-negotiables appear one by one with their minimum and full goal. Numbers can be adjusted; tasks can't be removed.
  3. Wake and sleep times: sets Aurora and the sleep log.
  4. Your step one (optional): name a goal and get a first tiny step.
  5. Your tone: Philosopher, Centurion or Roast me.
  6. The oath: a 20-second voice note on why you're doing this. It plays back on Day 10, when most people quit.
  7. Turn on the Vigil (optional, 1.0): a clear screen on what it reads and never reads, then the permission. Offered again on Day 2 if skipped.
  8. First selfie, then the VINCO stamp on Day 1.

The four non-negotiables

Each task has a minimum that keeps the day alive and a full goal that earns the laurel. A bad day stays a bad day instead of a broken campaign, which is when most people quit.

TaskHold the lineConquerHow it's checked
Water1 litre within 30 min of waking4 litres across the day, adjustable to body sizeOne tap per litre
Wake-upUp within 15 min of the alarmUp at the set time, mission done first tryAurora mission
Protein meal1 high-protein meal2, then 3 meals as you level upTap, optional photo
Workout15-minute walk or short home set40 minutes, gym or homeTap, then a 15-second voice note
  • Meal levels: after 5 days in a row at full goal, Vinco offers a promotion to two meals, then three. The user accepts or declines; it's never forced.
  • Voice note: stored on the phone. In 1.0 it is transcribed into a short workout log. A typed note is the fallback for quiet places.

The fifth task: step one

The user names a bigger goal ("crack my placement interview," "start a YouTube channel") and gets one step that takes under 15 minutes and can be done today. They can accept it, edit it or ask for another. When it's done, Vinco asks for the next step. Early Access uses templates; 1.0 uses AI.

Your own tasks and the Armory

Users can add any task. Custom tasks count toward the daily stamp only if marked as orders; otherwise they're just a list. The Armory offers ready-made tasks with sensible minimums:

Body

Skincare routine, haircare, 10,000 steps, stretching, cold shower, no sugar

Mind

Read 10 pages, journal 3 lines, 10 minutes of meditation, learn one new word

Work

1 hour of deep study, inbox to zero, practise a skill

Discipline

Phone out of the bedroom, morning sunlight, no screens after 11pm

Once a week, Vinco suggests one new Armory habit based on what the user already does well: "You've hit water 10 days straight. Ready to add morning sunlight?"

Aurora, the wake-up alarm

The alarm keeps ringing until the user completes a mission that gets them out of bed:

See the sky

Photograph the sky from a window or balcony, checked on the phone by an image check.

Reach the post

Scan a QR code printed and stuck in the bathroom or kitchen.

March

Walk 30 steps, counted by the phone's step sensor.

An "I'm sick" option stops the alarm but uses the week's Truce, so it can't become a lazy snooze. The mission's finish time is the real wake-up time for the sleep log.

The Vigil

Counts every reel and short as you watch it, shows the count live, and steps in at milestones. Ships in Vinco 1.0 for Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts on Android.

The idea is proven: apps on the Play Store already block reels and count scrolls through Android's Accessibility API, like AppBlock and digipaws. Vinco's edge is the counter tied to your arc, your tasks and your squad.

The floating badge

⚔ 23

Draggable and semi-transparent. Marble white, then gold, then porphyry red past your budget.

Tap the badge

  • Today: 63 reels, 47 min (+12 vs yesterday)
  • Squad top 3 today, plus your position
  • Leave the arena or Keep watching

1. Counting

  • Detects when a Reels or Shorts screen is open and counts each new video swiped to.
  • Tracks time spent on those screens.
  • Detection rules are downloaded as a small config file, so when Instagram changes its layout, Vinco is fixed without a new release.
  • Counts stay on the phone. Only daily totals go to the server, and only for users in a squad.

2. Friends ranking

Fewest reels wins. The top 3 are the most disciplined, so the ranking never rewards scrolling. Squads using Roast mode can opt into a playful "most reels" board too.

3. Milestones

The user picks the step (every 25 reels by default). At each milestone the feed pauses with a full-screen card that can be closed after 5 seconds. It escalates:

MilestoneWhat appears
25 reelsA Stoic quote, like Seneca's idea that life isn't short, we just waste much of it
50 reelsA 10 to 15 second nudge video, plus your open tasks: "Water: 1 of 4 litres"
75 reelsTime lost so far, turned into something real
100 reelsA firmer card in your tone, and the offer to set a limit for tomorrow

Showing open tasks at a milestone is the bridge between the counter and the tracker: the moment you notice you're scrolling is the best moment to remind you of something better.

4. Time lost

Shown in minutes and as something you could have done instead: "47 minutes: that's your full workout," "two chapters of a book," "a 5 km walk." The Senate report adds up the week: "6 hours, almost a full working day."

The privacy promise

The Vigil reads only whether a reels or shorts screen is open and when a new video starts. It never reads messages, captions, usernames, passwords or what the video is about, and nothing on screen is saved or sent. This goes on the permission screen, the store listing and the privacy policy, word for word.

Guard dialogues (1.1)

When the user opens an app they marked as a time sink (Instagram, a game, Chrome), a small Roman guard floats in with a line in their tone:

  • "Instagram, again? That's the 14th time today."
  • "The arena is open. Your workout is not done."
  • "Halt. State your business."

Then a three-tap intent check: Reply to someone, Look something up, Just bored. "Just bored" offers one open task instead. Asking why is often enough to stop the unconscious open, which is the real goal.

Imperium, strict mode (1.2)

  • Pick which apps to guard and set a limit by time or by reels.
  • At the limit, the app freezes until midnight.
  • Clementia: finishing all daily tasks earns one 10-minute unlock. Unlocks are always earned, never sold, so Vinco never profits from a user failing.
  • No self-sabotage: turning Imperium off takes effect after 24 hours.
  • Never blocked: calls, messages from contacts, payment apps, maps and anything marked essential. Blocking a payment or a family call is the fastest way to get uninstalled.

Tech stack

React Native with Expo, a Hono API, Postgres in Mumbai, Better Auth, and a Next.js admin, all in one TypeScript monorepo.

The decisions and why

App: React Native with Expo

You already know React, so you move fastest here. The Vigil, the floating badge and Aurora need native Android code, written as small Kotlin modules through Expo's module system. Use Expo development builds, not Expo Go, because Expo Go can't load custom native code.

Backend: Hono on Node, not Express

Hono is TypeScript-first, fast, has built-in validation with Zod, and runs on any host. Its typed client lets the app and admin call the API with autocomplete and type checking. Express works, but TypeScript support is bolted on rather than built in.

API style: REST, not GraphQL

GraphQL shines when many teams and clients need very different data. You're one developer with two clients you control. REST with Hono's typed client gives most of GraphQL's benefits with much less complexity.

Database: Postgres, hosted on Supabase in Mumbai

Supabase is used only as hosted Postgres, connected through Drizzle like any database. Neon is excellent, but its regions are in the US, Frankfurt, Singapore and Sydney (Neon docs), with no India region. Supabase has had a Mumbai region since late 2025 (source), which means faster responses and Indian user data staying in India.

Because it's plain Postgres and all logic lives in Hono, moving to Neon, AWS or any host later is a standard migration.

Auth: Better Auth, inside the Hono API

Users live in your own database, with no vendor lock-in. It supports everything Vinco needs (details below).

Selfies stay on the phone

They are the most sensitive data in the app and costly to host. Optional backup in 1.1 goes to the user's own Google Drive, in a hidden app folder.

Does Better Auth support Google and other logins?

Yes. From Better Auth's docs:

  • Expo support: an official Expo plugin, with sessions stored in the phone's secure storage (Expo integration docs).
  • Google sign-in: through a browser flow, or natively by passing Google's ID token from the phone's own sign-in sheet. Use the native one: it's one tap.
  • Phone number OTP: through its phone number plugin.
  • Also available: email OTP, magic links, passkeys, two-factor sign-in, other social providers, and an admin plugin for roles and banning users, which the admin dashboard uses.

Two things to plan for. Phone OTP needs an SMS provider, and sending SMS in India requires registering your sender ID and message templates on the DLT system, which can take days. Launch 1.0 with Google sign-in first and add phone OTP when DLT is approved. When iOS comes, Apple requires Sign in with Apple alongside other social logins; Better Auth supports it.

How it grows into global leaderboards and posts

  • Global leaderboard: the same daily totals table, with a job that rebuilds the top 100 every few minutes. Users read the cached board plus their own rank. If it slows down, add a Redis cache; the data model doesn't change.
  • Global posts: a posts table in Postgres, media in R2 behind a CDN, and moderation (automated image check plus user reports) before anything goes public.
  • Live features: start without websockets. Add them to Hono when live squad updates are worth it.
  • Heavier jobs: moderation queues and big push fan-outs run in a background worker using the same code and database.
  • Privacy law: India's DPDP Act needs clear consent and a delete-my-data option. Build the "delete my account" button from day one.

Privacy rules for admins

  • Selfies, voice notes and slip logs never reach the server, so admins can't see them.
  • Goals are stored as categories for stats; personal goal text stays on the phone.
  • Every time an admin opens a single user's details, it's written to the audit log.
